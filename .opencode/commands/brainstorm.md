Brainstorm: $ARGUMENTS

Goal: widen the option space before committing to requirements. Do not write
production code, scaffold a project, or make binding decisions.

- Frame the problem in one paragraph: who it is for, and what changes if it
  succeeds.
- Generate 3-5 genuinely different directions. For each, record the idea, what
  it makes easy, what it makes hard, and the evidence that would tell us it is
  working.
- Include one cheap experiment and one option that challenges the obvious
  answer.
- Separate known constraints from assumptions. Anything not confirmed is an
  assumption.
- Eliminate only options that violate a known constraint; do not rank by
  preference yet.

Write `docs/00-brainstorm.md`: problem frame, candidate directions, assumptions
to test first, and open questions.

Finish with the two or three directions worth grilling, then say:
`Choose a direction, or ask for more options.`
